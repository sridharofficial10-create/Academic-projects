# Passive Cooling System for Homes

**Date:** August 2025

## About the Project

This project explores how simple passive cooling techniques can help reduce heat inside a residential house without depending completely on active cooling systems.

A small house prototype was developed to study the effect of ventilation, reflective roofing, shading, and other design factors on indoor temperature. Temperature measurements were taken from both indoor and outdoor locations during testing.

## Objective

The main objective was to develop a low-cost residential house prototype that demonstrates passive cooling principles and allows indoor and outdoor temperature conditions to be compared.

## Key Features

- Natural and cross ventilation
- Reflective roofing to reduce heat absorption
- Shading considerations for reducing direct heat gain
- Comparison of indoor and outdoor temperatures
- Small-scale house prototype for practical testing

## Prototype and Development

The prototype house was designed and developed to demonstrate how building design can influence indoor thermal conditions.

SketchUp was used for the 3D design. An ESP32 with DHT22 sensors and an RTC was used for temperature monitoring and recording during the testing process.

## Testing

Temperature readings were observed at different times of the day, including morning, afternoon, and evening.

Indoor and outdoor conditions were compared to understand how the passive cooling features affected the temperature inside the prototype.

The prototype demonstrated the practical use of passive cooling principles and provided a way to compare indoor and outdoor temperature conditions during testing.

The project helped me understand how building design, ventilation, shading, and reflective surfaces can be considered together to improve thermal comfort.
